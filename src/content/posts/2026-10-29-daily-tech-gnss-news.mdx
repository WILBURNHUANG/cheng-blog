Wednesday was a day the market spent waiting, then reacting after everyone had gone home. The regular session was a study in restraint: the Federal Reserve delivered the hold almost everyone expected, stocks drifted up to within sight of records, and chips did the heavy lifting on blowout memory and database numbers. The real verdict came after the close, when the two biggest names in the AI buildout reported within minutes of each other and the market split them apart—rewarding the one whose spending still throws off cash and punishing the one whose doesn't. In positioning, the month's resilient-navigation theme kept finding new form factors: an anti-jam upgrade you bolt onto gear already in the field, and an inertial navigator small enough for a patrol boat.

## Tech News

### The Fed Holds Rates Steady as Stocks Edge to the Doorstep of Records

The central event was a non-event, and the market liked it that way. The **Federal Reserve** left its federal funds target range unchanged at **3.75% to 4%** in a **10-2** vote, with Governor **Lisa Cook** and Cleveland Fed President **Loretta Hammack** dissenting in favor of a quarter-point hike—a pause that follows an increase at the prior meeting, per TradingKey. With the decision in line with expectations, the indexes ground out modest gains: the **Nasdaq Composite** rose **0.27%** to **26,549.85**, the **S&P 500** added **0.28%** to **7,743.98**, and the **Dow Jones Industrial Average** gained **0.19%** to **52,475.10**.

The strength was concentrated in semiconductors ahead of the after-hours earnings deluge. The **Philadelphia Semiconductor Index** jumped **1.75%** to **11,674**, with 22 of its 30 members higher. Two post-earnings movers set the tone: **Oracle** surged **10.58%** to **$144.34** after projecting **$30 billion** of fiscal-2029 free cash flow and more than **10 gigawatts** of data-center capacity by the end of fiscal 2028, and **SK Hynix's** U.S.-listed shares leapt **13.3%** to **$164.70** on a quarter that showed revenue up **32%** sequentially and an operating margin of **79.7%**. The calm session was really the market holding its breath for the numbers that would land after 4 p.m.

### Alphabet Slides After Hours as Capex Swamps a Blowout Cloud Quarter; Microsoft Beats

The night's main event was a tale of two AI balance sheets. **Alphabet** fell about **8%** in after-hours trading despite **Google Cloud** revenue soaring **103%** to **$30.7 billion**—far ahead of the roughly 90% analysts expected. The problem was everything underneath it: total revenue of **$127.6 billion** (up 25%) slightly missed, EPS came in light, net income fell to **$26.5 billion** from **$35.0 billion** a year earlier, and capital spending ballooned to **$51.4 billion** from **$24.0 billion**, pushing quarterly free cash flow to **negative $9.1 billion**, per CNBC and earnings coverage. Investors who had cheered the AI buildout all month balked at seeing the cash it consumes laid out so starkly.

**Microsoft** drew the opposite reaction, rising more than **2%** after hours. Fiscal first-quarter revenue hit **$92.3 billion**, up **19%**, with adjusted EPS of **$4.89** beating the **$4.73** estimate and net income up **32%** to **$36.6 billion**. The number that mattered was **Azure**, which grew **48%**—well above the 44% consensus—while **Microsoft Cloud** revenue rose **30%** to **$63.9 billion**. Microsoft spent **$45.2 billion** on capital expenditures including finance leases, actually below the $50.7 billion feared, and still generated **$18.6 billion** of free cash flow even after a 27% year-over-year decline. The split verdict crystallized the quarter's central question: the market will pay for AI spending that still prints cash, and punish the same spending when it doesn't.

### Chips Rally as Memory and Database Names Reset the AI Hardware Bar

Beneath the megacap drama, the supply chain kept confirming that AI demand is real and accelerating. **SK Hynix's** results—revenue of **104.62 trillion won**, up 32% quarter-on-quarter, with operating profit of **83.33 trillion won**, up 38%—were a direct read on insatiable demand for the high-bandwidth memory that feeds AI accelerators, and the stock's 13% pop dragged the broader chip complex up with it. **Oracle's** multi-gigawatt capacity roadmap and $30 billion free-cash-flow target offered the same signal from the data-center side, reframing the database company as one of the largest buildout plays in the market.

The common thread is capacity commitments that stretch years into the future, echoing the multi-year visibility other hardware names have flagged this earnings season. For a tape that spent two weeks earlier in the month fretting that AI spending might not pay off, Wednesday's chip leadership was the counterpoint: the companies selling the picks and shovels keep beating and raising, even as the hyperscalers doing the digging get scrutinized line by line.

## GNSS News

### NAL Technologies Launches a Plug-In Iridium Antenna to Shield Fielded Gear from Jamming

The freshest positioning story came as a retrofit rather than a new box. **NAL Technologies (Naltec)** launched **PntGuard Syntrx**, a plug-and-play antenna upgrade that adds an assured positioning, navigation, and timing (APNT) signal to equipment already in service—protecting its GPS-based timing and location without hardware replacement or costly re-engineering, per Inside GNSS (**October 9**). Syntrx detects interference automatically and adjusts its output so a host system keeps running through jamming, denial, or spoofing.

The capability leans on a signal rather than better shielding. Syntrx draws on a secure **Iridium** PNT broadcast that Naltec says is roughly **1,000 times stronger** than GNSS and resistant to jamming and spoofing, the same low-Earth-orbit approach now appearing across the resilient-PNT market—here aimed squarely at gear that is already deployed. Target users span defense platforms such as tactical radios, satcom terminals, and electronic-warfare systems, plus critical-infrastructure operators in finance, energy, and telecom that depend on precise timing synchronization. "Nearly every fielded network, terminal, and weapon system depends on accurate PNT," said Naltec President **Robert Bills**, noting the company's APNT modules are already in more than **15,000** devices worldwide; commercial shipments of Syntrx begin in December.

### Exail Unveils the Compact Marins M1 Inertial Navigator for Small and Uncrewed Craft

The complementary story shrank high-end inertial navigation down to a hull fitting. **Exail** launched the **Marins M1**, a compact inertial navigation system built on the company's sea-proven **fiber-optic gyroscope (FOG)** technology and aimed at platforms that have historically been too small or cost-sensitive for a top-tier INS, per GPS World (**October 26**). The target list runs from rigid-hulled inflatable boats and fast interception craft to patrol boats, uncrewed surface vessels, and uncrewed underwater vehicles.

The pitch is accuracy that survives a lost satellite fix. The Marins M1 outputs position, heading, roll, and pitch with a maximum position drift of **1 nautical mile after 3 hours** with no external aiding—a figure meant to keep smaller and autonomous vessels on track when GNSS is degraded or jammed. Exail positions the system at a more accessible price point than comparable naval-grade units, part of a broader push to put resilient dead-reckoning navigation on the swarms of small crewed and uncrewed boats now entering maritime fleets, rather than reserving it for large warships.
