Wednesday's news orbited a single event—Nvidia's earnings—and the nervous market that greeted it, even as the print beat expectations. Underneath the headline, the AI economy kept rearranging itself: a record settlement over social media's effect on teenagers, a clearer picture of Google's custom-silicon ambitions through Marvell, and the quiet retirement of a two-decade-old crowdwork marketplace that helped train the machine-learning era. In positioning, the throughline moved from denial and defense to authentication: two receiver makers shipped products that let the hardware itself decide whether the satellite signal it hears is real.

## Tech News

### Update: Nvidia Beats With $96.2 Billion in Revenue—but Shares Wobble Until a 70% Growth Promise

**Nvidia** delivered the quarter Tuesday's preview had been building toward, reporting fiscal Q2 revenue of **$96.2 billion**—up **106%** year over year—and earnings of **$2.22** a share, more than double a year ago, per Kiplinger and The Motley Fool. The forecast topped Wall Street estimates, but shares initially slipped as investors fixated on whether the AI-spending surge is peaking rather than on the beat itself, per Bloomberg.

What steadied the stock was guidance, not the trailing numbers. CFO **Colette Kress** told analysts, "We expect to grow revenue by approximately **70%** in fiscal 2028," a signal that data-center demand still has a long runway, and Nvidia shares recovered after hours with benchmark futures ticking up about **1%** into Thursday, per Bloomberg. After a summer spent debating whether the buildout is sustainable, the market got its clearest data point yet—and still couldn't quite decide, underscoring how much of the AI trade now hinges on this one company's outlook.

### Meta Agrees to Pay $16.7 Billion to Settle Teen-Addiction Claims

**Meta** agreed to pay up to **$16.68 billion** to settle a landmark case alleging it deliberately designed **Facebook** and **Instagram** to hook teenagers, per Bloomberg and Tech Startups. The settlement is among the largest ever tied to social-media harm and closes one of the most consequential legal fronts the company has faced over its engagement-maximizing product design.

The size of the check is the message. For years, plaintiffs argued that features built to keep young users scrolling caused measurable harm, and courts increasingly took the claims seriously; the payout effectively prices that liability rather than litigating it further. It also arrives as Meta pours tens of billions into AI infrastructure, a reminder that the same attention-engineering that funds the buildout carries mounting legal and regulatory costs on the other side of the ledger.

### Marvell's $12.2 Billion Custom-Chip Deal With Google Comes Into Focus

Attention turned to **Marvell Technology** ahead of its **August 27** earnings, with investors seeking detail on the **$12.2 billion** custom-chip agreement it recently struck with **Google**, per The Motley Fool. Under the arrangement, Google obtained a warrant to buy nearly **59 million** Marvell shares—a stake that could dilute the chipmaker's outstanding shares by roughly **7%**.

The deal is a window into how hyperscalers are locking up bespoke silicon. Rather than rely solely on Nvidia GPUs, Google is deepening its custom-accelerator strategy and tying a key supplier's incentives directly to its own success through equity. For Marvell, an anchor customer of that scale validates its data-center franchise, but the warrant structure means Google captures upside as the partnership grows—another sign that the biggest buyers of AI compute are using their balance sheets to reshape the supply chain in their favor.

- **CrowdStrike raises its annual revenue forecast on AI-driven cyber threats**: **CrowdStrike** stock rose Wednesday after the security firm lifted its full-year revenue outlook above analyst estimates, citing demand fueled by artificial-intelligence-enabled attacks, per Bloomberg. Its retirement is a quiet marker of how far automated data pipelines have come since crowdsourced microtasks were the industry's backbone.

## GNSS News

### Trimble Launches RTX-NMA, the First Anti-Spoofing Solution for GPS and BeiDou

The most consequential positioning story of the day is authentication moving from a single constellation to three. **Trimble** introduced **RTX-NMA** (Navigation Message Authentication), which it calls the first solution on the market to mitigate spoofing attacks across both the **GPS** and **BeiDou** constellations, per Geo Week News. The capability leverages Trimble's **RTX** correction service to verify the integrity of GNSS navigation messages for all **ProPoint** receivers.

The significance is in the stacking. Used alongside **Galileo OSNMA**—the authentication scheme already broadcasting on Europe's constellation—Trimble customers now have three constellations protected against spoofing at once, a meaningful hedge in a year when falsified position data has plagued aviation, maritime, and survey users alike. Where much of 2026's GNSS-resilience story has been about navigating when signals are denied, message authentication attacks a different failure mode: making sure the signals a receiver does hear can be trusted before they steer anything.

### Septentrio Unveils the Ultra-Resilient mosaic-G5 P8 for Contested Environments

On the hardware side, **Septentrio**—part of **Hexagon**—launched the **mosaic-G5 P8**, a multi-frequency GNSS module measuring just **23 mm by 16 mm** and weighing **2.2 grams**, built for mission-critical use in UAVs, marine vessels, and rail, per EEJournal and Septentrio. Its **AIM+ Ultimate** technology is designed to protect the receiver against powerful and sophisticated jamming and spoofing, while surfacing interference indicators plus power and frequency data that can help localize a jammer.

What makes it notable is situational awareness packaged into a component. Rather than simply resisting an attack, the P8 reports on it—giving integrators the telemetry to see where interference is coming from, a feature that matters as jamming becomes routine in contested airspace and waters. It arrives alongside the company's **mosaic-G5 P6** precise-positioning module for commercial UAVs and robots, extending a family aimed at making resilience a default feature of the receiver rather than a bolt-on. Together with Trimble's authentication push, the day's GNSS news pointed to the same conclusion: the industry is building trust and interference-awareness directly into the silicon.
