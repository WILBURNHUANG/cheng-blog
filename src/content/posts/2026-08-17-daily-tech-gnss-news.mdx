Monday's tape had two currents pulling against each other. Underneath, the AI trade found a fresh reason to run—Anthropic's revenue is compounding fast enough to reset expectations for the whole sector—while on top, rising Treasury yields and firmer oil dragged the broad indexes lower and a central-bank blog reminded everyone how far the rally has stretched. The deal machine kept humming, from a payments giant buying an AI router to a video startup's vertical valuation leap. In positioning, the news split between building the next layer of Earth observation and coping with a navigation environment that keeps getting noisier over the Baltic.

## Tech News

### Anthropic's Revenue Run Rate Tops $65 Billion Ahead of Its IPO

**Anthropic**'s annualized revenue run rate has surpassed **$65 billion**, reached by the end of July, per Bloomberg and Yahoo Finance—up more than sevenfold from roughly **$9 billion** at the end of last year. The figure landed as the company behind Claude prepares a closely watched stock-market debut, and it was enough to move the market: chip stocks rose on Monday as the scale of Anthropic's growth stoked fresh optimism that AI demand is real and durable, per Bloomberg.

The number reframes the AI conversation. A run rate that size would, by some estimates, put Anthropic ahead of nearly every public software company on revenue pace, and it arrives while the firm reportedly spends far less than rivals to train its models. For investors who spent the summer worrying about how the AI buildout gets paid for, a model lab compounding revenue this fast is the cleanest counter-argument going—and it explains why semiconductors caught a bid even on a down day for the tape.

### Stripe Closes a $7 Billion Deal for AI Model-Router OpenRouter

**Stripe** has agreed to acquire **OpenRouter** for more than **$7 billion**, per Bloomberg and Fortune—a startup that gives developers a single access point to switch among AI models based on task, cost, and performance. The price is a striking markup: roughly **5.4x** the **$1.3 billion** valuation OpenRouter set in a Series B just three months earlier in May, per TechCrunch and Tech Startups.

The logic is infrastructure. Stripe already processes payments for OpenRouter, giving it a close read on the startup's transaction growth, and it plans to fold model routing into its broader "AI economic infrastructure," per the coverage. As Forkast framed it, the deal turns model selection into a payments-adjacent problem—metering and billing which model handles which request—another sign that the money layer of AI is consolidating as fast as the model layer.

### AI-Video Startup Higgsfield Quadruples Its Valuation to $5.4 Billion

**Higgsfield** raised a **$400 million** Series B at a **$5.4 billion** valuation, roughly quadrupling its worth in eight months from a **$1.3 billion** mark, per TechCrunch and PR Newswire. The round was led by **DST Global** with participation from **Goldman Sachs** Alternatives, **Intel** Capital, Liberty Global, Tribe Capital, and NTT DOCOMO Ventures, among others. Higgsfield touts **$700 million** in annualized revenue and **30 million** users across 200 countries.

The growth curve is the pitch. After the company's "Supercomputer" rollout in May, users of its agentic products grew **42-fold** in three months, driving more than **20 million** content generations a month, and Higgsfield says it now powers visual production for **390** of the Fortune 500, per its release. Coming amid a wave of AI-video launches, the raise is a bet that professional creators, brands, and studios—not just consumers—are the durable market for generative video.

## GNSS News

### PlanetiQ Clears a Build Milestone for Its Next-Gen GNSS Weather Satellites

The most forward-looking positioning story of the week is about sensing the atmosphere, not just navigating through it. **PlanetiQ** completed a major milestone for its next-generation **GNOMES** satellites, finishing manufacturing and testing and preparing to ship the spacecraft for launch integration "in the coming weeks," per GPS World and the company's release. The satellites carry advanced **GNSS radio occultation (GNSS-RO)** sensors, which measure how GNSS signals bend through the atmosphere to derive temperature, pressure, and moisture profiles that feed weather forecasting models.

The commercial pull is already there. PlanetiQ was awarded a **$2.73 million** contract by **NOAA** to deliver **4,200** GNSS-RO profiles and **500** total-electron-content tracks per day from September 18 through December 1, per PR Newswire—data prized for its accuracy and global coverage. It's a reminder that GNSS constellations are dual-use infrastructure: the same signals that guide receivers on the ground double as a global, all-weather sounding network overhead, and expanding that layer improves everything from storm tracking to space-weather awareness.

### Denmark Logs Record GPS Interference as Baltic Jamming Intensifies

On the resilience side, the contested-sky story keeps escalating. **Denmark** recorded unprecedented GPS interference around the island of **Bornholm**, with a measuring station logging **30** jamming and spoofing incidents so far in 2026—up from **16** in all of 2025 and **11** in 2024, per European Pravda and EU Today. The instruments can't confirm the source, but experts see reasonable grounds to suspect **Russia**; one cargo captain reported losing GPS for about a day on a Baltic transit.

The pattern is regional and worsening. Finland flagged frequent disruptions near the Åland Islands, Sweden reported a sharp rise in interference affecting civil aviation, and analysts estimate roughly **40%** of European air traffic now contends with some GNSS interference, per GPS World and related coverage—prompting the EU to announce sanctions targeting those behind the jamming. Flights aren't being cancelled outright—aircraft fall back to inertial and ground-based aids when GNSS degrades—but "occasional" interference has become "basically constant," which is exactly the operational reality driving demand for the resilient, multi-sensor PNT that has dominated this year's positioning news.
