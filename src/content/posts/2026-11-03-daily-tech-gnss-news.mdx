The AI-spending anxiety that gutted the back half of October did not take a holiday. Monday opened November with another tech-led slide, even as the Dow pushed higher in a now-familiar rotation out of crowded megacaps. A $115 billion chip merger landed with a thud, a database giant's capital raise reawakened fears about how all this AI infrastructure gets paid for, and oil reversed last week's war premium in a single session. Then the bell rang on strong results from Apple and Palantir, and the market turned toward an Election Day that could reshape the policy backdrop. In positioning, the month's resilience theme shifted to the oldest trick in navigation—radio beacons on the ground—and to a constellation that just got its fourth satellite back.

## Tech News

### The Tech-Led Selloff Carries Into November as the Nasdaq Slides Again

November began where October left off. On Monday the **Nasdaq Composite** fell **1.07%** to **25,164.32** and the **S&P 500** slipped **0.51%** to **7,541.40**, while the **Dow Jones Industrial Average** bucked the trend to rise **0.42%** to **51,989.19**, per TradingKey and StockTitan. The **Nasdaq-100** dropped **1.39%** to **27,989.82**, and [Bloomberg reported](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-02/nasdaq-100-enters-correction-as-megacap-results-amplify-ai-fears) the index had entered correction territory as megacap results amplified AI-spending fears.

The split tape told the story of the moment: money kept leaving the crowded AI winners for the rest of the market. The **Invesco QQQ** fell **1.36%** to **$681.09** while rate-sensitive and defensive corners held up, extending the rotation that defined the last two weeks of October. Investors were also positioning ahead of **Election Day on November 3**, with U.S. midterm results looming as a fresh source of policy uncertainty layered on top of the AI-capex debate.

### Marvell's $115 Billion Deal for Lumentum Lands With a Thud

The day's biggest corporate news was a megadeal the market hated. **Marvell** agreed to acquire optical-components maker **Lumentum** in an all-stock transaction valuing the target at roughly **$115 billion** in enterprise value, at a fixed exchange ratio of **4.5 Marvell shares per Lumentum share**—a **23.9%** premium to Lumentum's unaffected close on October 28, per reporting aggregated by TheWallStreetRepublic. Marvell shareholders would own about **67%** of the combined company, which management projected would generate more than **$1 billion** in annual cost synergies, with a termination fee of up to **$5.8 billion** (or **$3.2 billion** if regulators block the deal).

Instead of cheering the strategic logic, investors punished both stocks. **Marvell** tumbled **12.72%** to **$171.41** and **Lumentum** sank **18.59%** to **$684.40**—even though Lumentum beat on earnings with EPS of **$4.57** against a **$4.21** estimate. Because the offer is entirely in Marvell stock, Marvell's own plunge dragged the deal's implied per-share value down with it, turning what looked like a rich premium on paper into a moving target.

### Apple and Palantir Beat After the Bell

The after-hours session delivered two strong reports that drew muted reactions. **Apple** posted fiscal fourth-quarter revenue up **12%** to **$114.48 billion** with EPS of **$2.10** against a **$1.99** estimate, a record **$31.57 billion** in Services revenue, and a gross margin of **47.9%**. The stock had fallen **1.83%** to **$338.32** in the regular session and was little changed after hours once the numbers landed.

**Palantir** was the quarter's standout on growth. Third-quarter revenue jumped **96%** to **$2.31 billion**, ahead of the **$2.18 billion** consensus, as **U.S. commercial revenue surged 148%** to **$984 million**—overtaking the company's U.S. government revenue of **$953 million** for the first time—with GAAP EPS of **$0.44** and fourth-quarter guidance of **$2.649 billion to $2.653 billion**. Shares had slipped **3.13%** to **$178.34** during the day before edging up about **1.2%** in extended trading. In a tape fixated on whether AI spending is producing returns, both beats underscored demand even as the indexes sold off.

### Oracle Sinks on a Big Capital Raise as AI-Debt Fears Return

The sharpest single-name decliner outside the Marvell deal was a familiar AI buildout story. **Oracle** fell **8.76%** to **$119.22** after filing to raise roughly **$20 billion** through new stock and bond sales—including about **$10 billion** of common stock—to fund its data-center expansion, per StockTitan and TheWallStreetRepublic. The size of the raise revived worries about how much debt the AI infrastructure boom is piling up, a concern that has dogged the sector since late October.

The anxiety rippled straight into the chip complex, where **AMD** dropped **7.52%** and **Micron** fell **5.78%** on the session. Coming on the same day Marvell's megadeal soured, Oracle's raise reinforced the market's new reflex: reward AI demand, but flinch at the invoice and the borrowing it takes to meet it.

- **Markets brace for Election Day**: Wall Street headed into the **November 3 U.S. midterm elections** with futures cautious, adding a political wild card to an already jittery AI-driven tape.

## GNSS News

### R-Mode: The Radio Beacon Being Revived as a Satellite-Free GPS Backup

The freshest positioning story looked backward to move forward. In a widely circulated November 2 analysis by **James Crawford**, a former Chilean representative on the IALA Council, **R-Mode (Ranging Mode)** is framed as a serious terrestrial backup to GNSS: the method reuses existing **medium-frequency marine radio beacons**, synchronizing the stations precisely and adding two continuous-wave carriers so a shipborne receiver can measure phase, estimate range, and fix its position from three or more stations with no satellites involved. The driver is not accuracy but availability and integrity—exactly what jamming and spoofing erode.

The groundwork is further along than the beacons' vintage suggests. **Germany, Sweden and Poland** modified existing stations to build a Baltic testbed, and in **2021** medium-frequency R-Mode delivered positioning at sea for the first time, with horizontal accuracy better than **12 meters** (95%) in the central area between three transmitters. **IALA** standardized the signal in **Guideline G1187** in December 2024, and the **IMO's NCSR 13** finalized draft performance standards for shipborne R-Mode receivers in June 2026—headed to **MSC 112 in December 2026**. The push follows a June 2025 IMO warning about surging GNSS interference in European waters, with **South Korea's** three **eLoran** stations covering the Yellow Sea cited as a complementary terrestrial backup already in service.

### Update: India's NVS-03 Reaches Orbit, Restoring NavIC to Minimum Strength

The NavIC launch flagged as still-unconfirmed in the last digest has a result: it worked. ISRO's **GSLV-F18**, on the rocket's **20th flight**, lifted off at **11:51 p.m.** on October 30 from Sriharikota and injected the **2,232-kilogram NVS-03** satellite into a geosynchronous transfer orbit with a planned perigee of **170 km** and apogee of **37,023 km**, per Business Standard. All three stages—including the cryogenic upper stage—performed as predicted, and the spacecraft separated about **18 minutes** after liftoff.

The genuinely new detail beyond the launch itself is the payoff. ISRO will now use the satellite's onboard propulsion to raise it to its final slot at **32.5° East**, and the mission restores **NavIC** to its minimum operational configuration of **four satellites**—closing the gap left when the earlier NVS-02 suffered an orbit-raising shortfall. ISRO noted that roughly **80%** of the mission budget went to Indian industry, underscoring how much of the country's regional navigation program is now built at home.
