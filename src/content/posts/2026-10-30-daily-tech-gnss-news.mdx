Thursday was the day the AI-spending anxiety stopped being a one-night earnings reaction and became a full session. The regular tape sold off as the market kept punishing capital expenditure it had cheered all month, dragging the Nasdaq down more than a percent while defensive sectors and small caps went the other way—a classic rotation out of crowded megacaps. Then the bell rang and a fresh batch of reports landed, extending the same split verdict: the cloud business that is reaccelerating got sold on the cash it burns, and the chipmaker that cannot find growth got hammered on its guidance. In positioning, the month's resilience thread moved from hardware launches toward the plumbing and the policy—an expanding ecosystem of tiny GNSS modules for autonomous systems, and a sobering federal audit of how slowly the Pentagon is fielding its GPS replacements.

## Tech News

### Amazon's AWS Reaccelerates to 39%, but the Stock Slides on the Cost of AI

The night's biggest number was a growth rate nobody expected to see again. **Amazon** reported that **AWS** revenue rose **39%** to **$45.98 billion** in the third quarter—its fastest expansion in nearly five years, and an acceleration that puts the cloud unit growing as quickly as it did in 2021 at almost three times the size, per 24/7 Wall St. and GeekWire. CEO **Andy Jassy** told investors he expects AWS to keep growing above **40%** in both 2027 and 2028.

Yet the shares fell between **6% and 7%** in after-hours trading, because the rest of the report showed what that growth costs. Total revenue of **$201.94 billion** came in a hair light against the **$202.2 billion** estimate, GAAP EPS of **$1.89** missed the **$1.96** consensus, and trailing free cash flow slipped below zero on a **2026** capital-spending bill of roughly **$220 billion**, most of it for AI data centers. Management guided holiday-quarter sales to a range of about **$240 billion to $247 billion** (**12% to 16%** growth). It was the same trade the market made with the other hyperscalers this week—reward the demand, flinch at the invoice.

### Intel Craters ~17% as Weak Guidance Overshadows a Data-Center Surge

The sharpest after-hours move cut against a turnaround story. **Intel** fell nearly **17%** in extended trading after posting third-quarter revenue of **$16.5 billion** and guiding fourth-quarter revenue to just **$15.0 billion to $16.0 billion**—a sequential decline and well below Wall Street's hopes, per reporting aggregated by StockTitan. The chief financial officer warned that the PC slowdown is likely to persist "at least through the first half of next year," pouring cold water on a stock that had already been volatile.

The frustration is that one part of the business is clearly working. Intel's **data-center chip sales jumped 78%**, a sign it is finally capturing some of the AI server demand lifting its rivals. But with the client-computing engine stalling and the foundry buildout still a drag, investors judged the guidance, not the bright spot—extending a run that had already seen the stock whipsaw around a reported 20% selloff earlier in the month.

### Alphabet Logs Its Worst Day Ever as the Tape Rotates Out of Big Tech

The regular session told the other half of the story. The **Nasdaq Composite** fell **1.38%** to **26,184.72** and the **S&P 500** slipped **0.30%** to **7,720.39**, snapping a three-day winning streak, while the **Dow** eased **0.25%** to **52,342.56**, per cappnotes. Driving the damage was **Alphabet**, which sank **12.64%**—described as its worst single day on record—as the market fully digested the prior night's first double miss since 2022 and negative free cash flow of roughly **$9.1 billion**. **Caterpillar** compounded the pain on the Dow, dropping **9.25%**, its worst day since March 2020, on light construction sales despite an earnings beat.

The tell was underneath the headline indices. The **Russell 2000** rose **1.01%** to **2,859.22**, and the day's leaders were **health care** (**+2.40%**), **real estate** (**+2.21%**), and **consumer staples** (**+1.54%**), while **technology** (**-1.18%**) and **communication services** (**-1.87%**) were the only sectors lower. It was a clean rotation: with the AI megacaps suddenly a source of risk rather than only reward, money moved toward the rate-sensitive and defensive corners of the market that had been left behind all year.

**Additional Headlines:**

- **Reddit beats but guides light, falls 12.6%**: **Reddit** grew third-quarter revenue **53%** to **$895 million** and beat on EPS at **$1.40**, but shares dropped **12.6%** to **$113.04** after fourth-quarter revenue and EBITDA guidance came in below consensus and domestic daily active visitors slipped **1.7%** to **50.7 million**.
- **Coinbase revenue goes flat as prediction markets scale**: **Coinbase** reported roughly flat revenue quarter over quarter as spot trading volume fell **10%**, even as its newer **prediction markets** business crossed a **$200 million** annualized run rate and services reached about **49%** of net revenue.
- **The AI-capex verdict is now the whole market's story**: With Alphabet, Amazon, and Meta all penalized this week for the cash their buildouts consume, the debate has shifted from whether AI spending will pay off to when—and the market is repricing the names doing the heaviest digging.
- **Defensive rotation widens market breadth**: For a tape that spent the year narrowly led by a handful of megacaps, Thursday's rising small caps and defensive sectors offered a rare session where the average stock outperformed the index.

## GNSS News

### Septentrio Expands Its mosaic-G5 Ecosystem for Drones and Autonomous Systems

The freshest positioning story was about building blocks rather than a single box. **Septentrio**, now part of **Hexagon**, said it is expanding the ecosystem around its ultra-compact **mosaic-G5** GNSS receiver modules—devices measuring just **23 × 16 mm** and weighing as little as **2.2 grams**—to speed development of resilient, high-accuracy positioning for UAVs, robotics, and autonomous systems, per GPS World (**October 5**). The push adds commercial boards from partners including SparkFun, ArduSimple, ARK Electronics, Holybro, and newcomers Hoverfly and CUAV, alongside a set of open-source reference designs.

The strategy is to lower the barrier to entry for developers working under tight size, weight, and power budgets. Septentrio published GitHub reference designs—**mosaicG5-HAT**, **TeenG5**, **moTi**, and **G5Dot**—built to drop onto **NVIDIA**, **Raspberry Pi**, **Arduino**, **Teensy**, and **M.2** platforms, giving integrators a running start rather than a blank schematic. "The value of technology grows with the community and ecosystem around it," said Septentrio's **Gustavo Lopez**—a bet that putting survivable, centimeter-grade positioning within reach of small-platform designers accelerates the whole autonomy market.

### A New GAO Audit Details How Far the Pentagon's GPS Modernization Has Slipped

The complementary story came from the oversight side of the house, and it was blunt. A **Government Accountability Office** report released **October 7** (**GAO-27-107676**, *"Precision Navigation Systems: Challenges Remain with Fielding New Capabilities"*) found that the Defense Department's next-generation GPS and complementary PNT programs are running years behind, per Inside GNSS. Deliveries of the first ten **GPS IIIF** satellites have slipped an average of **nine months**, pushing the first launch to a projected **July 2027**, and GAO warned the schedule may not meet the requirement for **24 operational M-code satellites**. On the ground, DOD terminated the troubled **OCX** control system in April 2026 in favor of modernizing the existing one, with most capabilities not expected until **2028–2029**.

The report also sketched how urgently the alternatives are needed, citing a Secure World Foundation analysis that jamming in Ukraine cut one precision-guided munition's effectiveness by up to **80%**. Complementary efforts are advancing unevenly—the Army's **ALTNAV** reached initial capability in late 2024 but needs ground stations authorized by multiple countries, while military **M-code user equipment (MGUE)** is trickling onto platforms with full Army aviation fielding not projected until **2049**. GAO's central finding was institutional: fragmented service-by-service planning and budgets are slowing the whole enterprise, and it recommended the Army, Navy, and Air Force each assess consolidating their GPS and alt-PNT work—recommendations DOD concurred with.
