The AI trade found its feet again, this time on a day the bond market was dark. On Veterans Day, with Treasuries closed, stocks staged a broad rebound that carried the Nasdaq all the way back above where it sat before last week's turmoil—led, for once, not by the chip bellwethers but by the neocloud that had spent the prior week as a lightning rod. Yet the rally shared the tape with a governance story that kept getting worse: the most valuable company in the market rose in the regular session and then fell apart after the close as a second scandal broke over its CEO. In positioning, the month's resilience theme held, moving from the antenna bench to the regulatory docket—new hardware to beat jammers, and a renewed pitch to build a terrestrial safety net under GPS itself.

## Tech News

### AI Stocks Rebound on Veterans Day as the Nasdaq Erases Its Post-Election Losses

The bounce was broad and it stuck. On Wednesday the **Nasdaq Composite** rose **1.60%** (**+400.31**) to **25,466.19**, enough to erase the losses it had piled up since the midterm selloff began, while the **S&P 500** climbed **1.31%** (**+98.45**) to **7,619.38** and the **Dow Jones Industrial Average** added **0.49%** (**+254.60**) to **52,486.49**, per market data from Yahoo Finance. With the **bond market closed** for Veterans Day, equities traded without the steady drumbeat of yield moves that had whipsawed them all week, and the calmer backdrop let the AI complex do the talking.

The leadership marked a shift. Rather than the semiconductor bellwethers that had driven Monday's rebound, the charge was led by the AI-infrastructure names that had been hit hardest during the unwind—a sign that investors were buying back the very corner of the trade they had fled days earlier. Memory and chip names piled on, with **SanDisk**, **Micron** and **SK Hynix** each gaining more than **10%** and **AMD**, **Broadcom** and **Marvell** all higher, leaving the rally looking less like a defensive crouch and more like a return of risk appetite.

### CoreWeave Powers the Rally as the Neocloud Trade Roars Back

The day's engine was the stock the market had spent a week doubting. **CoreWeave** soared **24.71%** to **$60.77**, with investors seizing on a detail that reframed the bull case: the company is expecting roughly **$9 billion** in customer prepayments, a cash infusion that eases the funding worries hanging over the capital-hungry GPU-rental model, per market coverage of the move. The surge clawed back a large chunk of the ground CoreWeave had lost during the AI-infrastructure rout and turned the most-shorted idea of the prior week into the session's biggest winner.

The enthusiasm radiated across the AI-power and neocloud complex. **Bloom Energy** rose about **16%** as power-infrastructure stocks rallied on the read-through for data-center demand, and fellow neocloud **Nebius** jumped **15%** in early trading. For a market that had spent the previous week litigating whether the AI capital cycle had peaked, a 25% move in the most exposed name was the clearest sign yet that the dip-buyers had decided the buildout story was not broken—just oversold.

### Update: Nvidia Pops, Then Plunges After Hours as a Second Scandal Hits Jensen Huang

The market's bellwether got no relief. **Nvidia** closed the regular session up **2.92%** at **$173.73**, snapping a two-day losing streak—and then unraveled after the bell, falling **8.65%** to **$158.70** as two new reports landed. The **New York Times** reported that CEO **Jensen Huang** had a second extramarital affair, this one with Nvidia executive **Kimberly Powell**, who runs the company's healthcare business, describing the pair being seen together at a company conference in March. Hours later, the **Wall Street Journal** reported that Nvidia's board is **preparing to oust Huang** and was meeting to decide whether to do so.

The news turned an existing governance cloud into a leadership crisis. The reports followed Huang's earlier admission—in a Sunday email to employees—of a relationship with a woman suspected of being a foreign intelligence contact, the matter already under an independent board investigation. The market's verdict was swift: **Baird** downgraded the stock to Neutral, and with the after-hours drop, analysts pegged Nvidia down roughly **14%** for November, a slide that has wiped out on the order of **$700 billion** in market value. Having anchored the year's gains, the chipmaker now anchors its single largest risk—one that has nothing to do with chips.

**Additional Headlines:**

- **Memory stocks lead the chip surge**: Beyond the index leaders, memory makers were the standouts, with **SanDisk**, **Micron** and **SK Hynix** each climbing more than **10%** as the AI-storage trade snapped back alongside the broader semiconductor complex.
- **Quantum names stay two-sided**: **D-Wave** rose after reporting third-quarter revenue of about **$4.5 million**, up **21%** year over year, though its loss per share missed estimates—a reminder that the speculative edges of the AI trade are still sorted print by print.
- **Bitcoin lags the risk-on tape**: Even as equities rallied, **Bitcoin** hovered near **$73,700**, close to a three-month low, a sign that the recovery in stocks had not yet pulled the most speculative assets back up with it.
- **Commodities hold firm**: **WTI crude** traded around **$87** a barrel and **gold** held near **$4,050** an ounce, steady against the equity rebound after the prior week's sharp swings.

## GNSS News

### Taoglas Releases an Anti-Jam CRPA Antenna Reference Design at electronica

The freshest positioning story handed engineers a head start on beating jammers. On **November 10**, **Taoglas** released a **controlled reception pattern antenna (CRPA)** reference design for OEMs and system integrators building GNSS systems that must resist jamming, spoofing, interference and multipath, unveiling it at **electronica** in Munich (November 10–13), per Inside GNSS. Rather than a finished product, the design is a starting point built on commercial off-the-shelf parts and shipped with design files—covering the passive antenna and active electronics while leaving the beamforming and anti-jam processing to the integrator's own receiver and platform.

The architecture is modular by design. It pairs Taoglas's multi-band GNSS patch antennas—the **Accura GVLB258.A** (L1/L5), **HP2258.A** (L1/L2) and **HP25258.A** (L1/L2/L5)—with **TFM.110A** front-end modules, together covering GPS, Galileo, GLONASS and BeiDou, and Taoglas says a **four-element array** built on the design can form up to **three independent nulls** to steer reception away from interference. The company is targeting drones and other autonomous systems, mission-critical navigation, surveying, precision agriculture, maritime, public safety and GNSS timing. The design gives engineers "a practical, adaptable starting point" for anti-jam systems tailored to specific applications, said **Baha Badran**, Taoglas's global head of engineering, framing it as a way to cut development risk and shorten evaluation time for the hardest parts—element design, isolation, calibration and beamforming.

### NextNav Presses Its Case for a Terrestrial 5G Backup to GPS

The complementary story moved from the antenna to the policy docket. In its **Q3 2026** investor update on **November 10**, **NextNav** renewed its pitch to build a nationwide terrestrial complement to GPS, arguing the satellite system's vulnerability to jamming, spoofing and natural disruption demands a backstop that does not exist today, per an Investing.com summary of the company's slides. NextNav proposes a **5G-based positioning, navigation and timing (PNT)** system running in the **Lower 900 MHz band**, aligned with 3GPP standards and engineered to work indoors and in urban canyons where GPS struggles—delivered, it says, by partnering with existing 5G carriers rather than building standalone infrastructure.

The economic argument is the lever. NextNav points to a Brattle Group analysis estimating the value of a terrestrial GPS backup at **$14.6 billion**, against a U.S. economic cost of a GPS outage it pegs at **$1.6 billion for a single day** and **$58.2 billion over 30 days**. CEO **Mariam Sorond** cited Santa Clara field tests showing "single-digit meter positioning performance for both indoor and outdoor," and framed PNT resiliency as a bipartisan priority while acknowledging the timeline is uncertain. The regulatory path remains slow: the **FCC** adopted a 4-0 Notice of Inquiry in March 2025 and a proposed rulemaking on PNT technologies has sat under interagency review at the White House's OIRA since July 2025. Financially, NextNav reported Q3 revenue of **$1.58 million**—about **55%** above estimates—a narrower-than-expected loss, and roughly **$290.9 million** in cash as a debt-free balance sheet to fund the wait.
