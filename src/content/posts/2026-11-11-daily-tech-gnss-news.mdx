The AI trade's two-day recovery ran straight into the inflation data. After Monday's forceful bounce, a hotter-than-expected October CPI report Tuesday pulled the rate-cut story off the table, lifted bets on another Fed hike in December, and sent the indexes back into the red—led, as ever, by Nvidia. Underneath the pullback the earnings tape stayed violently two-sided, with single names swinging 50% and more in both directions, and after the close an AI-cloud heavyweight delivered a blowout quarter that kept the infrastructure debate alive. In positioning, the week's GNSS news stayed fixed on resilience, this time in the unglamorous but critical layer of timekeeping—and in the test benches built to break receivers before the jammers do.

## Tech News

### A Hot CPI Report Derails the Monday Bounce

The relief rally lasted exactly one session. On Tuesday the **Nasdaq Composite** fell **0.74%** to **25,065.88**, the **S&P 500** slipped **0.38%** to **7,520.93**, the **Dow Jones Industrial Average** dropped **0.30%** (**-157.86**) to **52,231.89**, and the small-cap **Russell 2000** lost **0.68%** to **2,821.38**, per market data from Yahoo Finance. The trigger was the delayed **October Consumer Price Index**, which rose **0.4%** month over month at the headline level versus the **0.3%** economists expected, while **core CPI** climbed **0.3%** against a **0.2%** forecast—a reading warm enough to reignite worries that inflation is reaccelerating rather than cooling.

The data reset the Fed debate in a hawkish direction. Traders pushed the probability of a **25-basis-point hike** at the December meeting to about **84%**, and for the first time priced in a **15%** chance of a **50-basis-point** move, up from essentially zero the day before. Fed officials reinforced the tilt: Governor **Christopher Waller** said he leans toward a quarter-point hike and would consider a half-point if core inflation ran around 0.3% or higher, while Dallas Fed President **Lorie Logan** said two more hikes will be needed. The **10-year Treasury yield** pushed back above **5%**, and even a bright spot for consumers—AAA's national average gasoline price dipping below **$4** a gallon for the first time since July—did little to lift the mood on Wall Street.

### Nvidia Drags the Indexes as Its Market Cap Slips Below $4 Trillion

The day's heaviest weight was its usual one. **Nvidia** fell **5.47%** to **$168.89**, enough to drag all three major indexes lower on its own and to briefly push the company's market value back below **$4 trillion**, per CNBC. The chipmaker remains caught between the macro repricing and an idiosyncratic overhang: its board has now hired an outside law firm to conduct the independent investigation into reports about CEO **Jensen Huang**'s ties to a suspected foreign intelligence contact, formalizing a probe it opened earlier in the week.

The combination made Nvidia a clean read on the market's fragility. On a day when a hot inflation print would have pressured rate-sensitive growth stocks anyway, the single name most responsible for the year's gains carried both the macro risk and a governance cloud—leaving it as the largest drag on a tape that was already leaning lower. With the stock's year-to-date advance steadily eroded over the past two weeks, investors have little cushion left to absorb more bad headlines.

### CoreWeave Posts a Breakout Quarter as the AI-Infrastructure Debate Rages

After the bell, the AI buildout got a fresh data point. **CoreWeave** reported third-quarter revenue of **$3.66 billion**, up roughly **168%** from a year earlier, and lifted its full-year 2026 revenue guidance to a range of **$13.0 billion to $13.3 billion**, per its SEC filing. **Adjusted EBITDA** reached **$2.26 billion**—a **62%** margin—and the company reported a revenue backlog of about **$132.5 billion** as of September 30, a staggering pipeline that underscores how much committed AI demand is sitting behind the neocloud model. Shares rose about **2.6%** in after-hours trading.

The print did not settle the bull-bear fight so much as sharpen it. Against the record revenue sat a net loss of **$709 million**, widened from **$110 million** a year earlier, a reminder that the GPU-rental business is still spending enormous sums to grow into that backlog. "This was a breakout quarter for CoreWeave," CEO **Michael Intrator** said, arguing the company's financial flexibility "positions us to keep delivering the infrastructure and software customers need to build, run, and scale AI." For a market that spent last week litigating whether the AI capital cycle has peaked, a 168% growth rate paired with a widening loss was exactly the kind of result both sides could claim.

## GNSS News

### Safran Launches StarSync U4, a Resilient Time Server Built for High-Speed Networks

The freshest positioning story moved away from satellites and onto the clocks that keep networks in sync. On **November 3**, **Safran Electronics & Defense** launched the **StarSync U4**, a next-generation time server and interoperability platform aimed at high-speed Ethernet networks, per Inside GNSS. The unit delivers **sub-nanosecond synchronization** through Safran's native **White Rabbit** implementation and combines White Rabbit, **PTP** and **NTP** in a single modular platform, so operators can fold multiple timing technologies into existing systems rather than ripping them out. The hardware adds a new processing architecture and four high-speed **1G/10G SFP+** interfaces.

The resilience angle is the point. StarSync U4 pairs **multi-source reference monitoring**, redundancy and seamless failover with integrated GNSS, enabling UTC-traceable timing from the reference source all the way to end equipment—so that when one timing source is lost or degraded, downstream clocks do not drift and disrupt operations. Safran is targeting financial trading, data centers, network monitoring, critical infrastructure, defense, aerospace and emerging quantum systems. "This solution brings the proven precision of Safran's White Rabbit implementation into a versatile time server platform," said **Carlos Valenzuela**, senior product manager at Safran Electronics & Defense, adding that the design lets customers "build highly accurate and reliable timing infrastructures" that scale with their needs.

### Rohde & Schwarz Builds a Bench for Breaking Receivers Before the Jammers Do

The complementary story came from the test lab. At **ION GNSS+ 2026**, Rohde & Schwarz application engineer **Paul Shaffer** detailed to Inside GNSS how the company gives receiver developers a controlled environment to probe exactly how their products hold up against jamming and spoofing. The core instruments are the **R&S SMW200A**—a vector signal generator that supports multi-constellation, multi-frequency and multi-antenna scenarios and can be combined into phase-coherent, multi-channel configurations—and the more compact **R&S SMBV100B** for receiver characterization and production testing. Both can simulate difficult real-world conditions such as urban driving while layering in controlled, adjustable interference, and can replay recorded I/Q data or stream custom waveforms.

The value is repeatability. In a demonstration, a supplied u-blox receiver gets a fix and then loses it as interference rises, letting engineers pinpoint the precise conditions where tracking and positioning fail—a controlled way to establish a receiver's breaking point before it is deployed into an environment where interference is no longer a lab setting. Rohde & Schwarz is also developing AI-assisted testing tools: its **AI Workplace** uses **Model Context Protocol (MCP)** servers to connect AI agents with instrument interfaces, documentation and automation, currently on the CMX500 radio tester with more instruments in development. "Controlled, reliable signals" that reveal how equipment behaves in the real world, Shaffer argued, are what resilience evaluation now demands.
