The week that nearly broke the AI trade ended with a shaky truce. On Friday the chip names that had led Thursday's rout bounced hard, dragging the Nasdaq back into the green even as the broader tape stayed cautious and the Dow slipped again. But the day's real story came from the data desk, not the trading floor: a long-delayed October jobs report landed far stronger than anyone expected, yanking rate-cut hopes off the table and sending the dollar up and gold down. Overseas, Asia was still catching up to Thursday's panic, with Tokyo and Seoul down roughly 6%. In positioning, the month's resilience theme carried on in miniature and at sea—a four-gram module that sniffs out jamming, and an atomic clock built to keep time when the satellites go quiet.

## Tech News

### AI Stocks Halt the Rout, but Wall Street Still Logs Its Worst Week Since June

The bleeding stopped—mostly. On Friday the **Nasdaq Composite** rose **1.09%** to **24,579.42** and the tech-heavy **Nasdaq-100** climbed **1.35%** to **26,989.99**, while the **S&P 500** added **0.31%** to **7,376.86** and the small-cap **Russell 2000** gained **0.89%** to **2,754.11**, per cappnotes. The **Dow Jones Industrial Average** was the holdout, slipping **0.46%** (**-237.33**) to **51,285.73**. The rebound was led by the same semiconductors that had been gutted a day earlier: **AMD** surged **11.04%**, **Micron** rose about **5.1%**, **Coherent** jumped **13.58%** and **Extreme Networks** climbed **14.16%**, with technology the day's best sector at **+1.45%**.

The relief did not undo the damage. The Nasdaq still ended the week down about **3.4%** and remained in correction territory, the **S&P 500** fell roughly **2.7%** on the week, and the Dow lost about **0.9%**—Wall Street's worst week since June. Breadth told the real story of a narrow bounce: just **182** advancers against **312** decliners, with the equal-weight **RSP** down **0.38%** even as the cap-weighted indexes rose. The gains were concentrated in the AI complex that had fallen the furthest, not a broad vote of confidence.

### The Delayed October Jobs Report Blows Past Forecasts and Kills Rate-Cut Hopes

The week's pivot was economic, not political. The long-delayed **October nonfarm payrolls** report finally landed and showed the economy added **165,000** jobs—nearly triple the roughly **59,000** consensus—while the **unemployment rate** fell to **4.1%** from **4.2%**, per Schwab and cappnotes. Average hourly wages rose **0.4%** month over month, and prior months were revised up by a combined **84,000**, painting a labor market far warmer than the slowdown investors had penciled in.

Markets reacted by repricing the Fed. Futures-implied odds of a **December rate hike** jumped to roughly **78%**, up from about **71%** a week earlier, and the move rippled across assets: the **dollar index** rose **0.76%** to **102.32**, **Treasury yields** climbed with the 2-year up **5 basis points** to **4.51%** and the 10-year up to **4.86%**, and **gold** reversed its haven run to fall **0.79%** to about **$4,081** an ounce. With the October **CPI** report due the following Tuesday, a hot jobs print turned the macro backdrop from a tailwind into a fresh source of risk.

### Microsoft Weighs on Tech as the "Neocloud" Trade Splits

Beneath the chip rebound, a quieter repricing of the AI-infrastructure supply chain played out. **Microsoft** fell about **1.0%** on reports that it plans to rely on **fewer neoclouds**—the specialized GPU-rental providers that rent out AI compute—a signal that the hyperscalers may pull more of their capacity in-house. The news cut against the grain of the day's recovery and kept the Dow in the red.

The neoclouds themselves, however, rebounded. **CoreWeave** rose about **6.3%**, clawing back part of its brutal week ahead of its own earnings report due the following week. The divergence captured the market's unresolved question heading into the back half of November: even as investors bid the chipmakers back up, they are still sorting out which middlemen in the AI buildout actually get paid—and which get disintermediated by the very customers funding them.

- **Defensive sectors lag the bounce**: Consumer staples (**-1.28%**) and materials (**-0.73%**) fell as money rotated back toward growth, the mirror image of the flight-to-safety trade that had dominated the week's selloff.

## GNSS News

### u-blox Packs a GNSS Receiver and Antenna Into a 4.4-Gram Module

The freshest hardware story shrank resilient positioning down to the size of a coin. On **November 5**, **u-blox** launched the **SAM-M11N**, a GNSS module measuring **15.5 × 15.5 × 5.3 mm** and weighing just **4.4 grams**—a millimeter thinner and nearly a gram lighter than its predecessor—that integrates a high-gain, right-hand circularly polarized patch antenna directly on the package, per Inside GNSS. Built on the company's **u-blox F11** platform, it delivers **1.5-meter CEP** horizontal accuracy and update rates up to 25 Hz, while a new **Low Energy Accurate Positioning (LEAP)** mode draws as little as **10 milliwatts**. "Every millimeter, gram and milliwatt matters," said Stefania Sesia, who heads u-blox's Standard Precision and Low Power GNSS business unit.

The security angle is what ties it to the month's theme. Unlike the ROM-based SAM-M10Q it replaces, the M11N ships with embedded flash so its firmware—including positioning algorithms—can be updated in the field without swapping hardware, and it adds onboard **detection and reporting of RF interference, jamming and spoofing**. Aimed at asset tags, livestock and personal trackers, lightweight drones and aftermarket telematics, the module pushes jamming awareness into the smallest, most cost-sensitive corner of the market, with first samples due at **electronica 2026** in Munich, November 10–13.

### Infleqtion Runs an Optical Atomic Clock at Sea During an AUKUS Naval Exercise

The complementary story went to the other extreme—keeping precise time when GPS is gone entirely. In an account published **November 6**, Inside GNSS reported that **Infleqtion** ran its **Tiqker** optical atomic clock continuously for more than two weeks aboard the U.S. Navy support vessel **RV Merlin** during the **Maritime Big Play Pacific (MBP-P)** exercise, an **AUKUS Pillar II** demonstration led by the U.S. Navy with Australian and UK partners that ran October 5–25 in the Southern California operating area. The clock survived large temperature swings and sustained vibration at sea, a step toward fielding lab-grade optical timing outside the lab.

The purpose is resilient synchronization. Infleqtion says Tiqker can hold precise local time when GPS is unavailable, jammed or spoofed, keeping a ship's communications, radar and other systems in lockstep without a satellite reference. "The Navy needs dependable timing to navigate, communicate and coordinate operations in the field," said CEO Matthew Kinsella. The MBP-P run, which drew on more than **40 technologies** across the three AUKUS nations and also supported the subsea-warfare exercise BARB-26, follows earlier Tiqker trials aboard the Royal Navy's uncrewed submarine testbed XV Excalibur in 2025 and UK flight demonstrations in 2024.
