October went out the way the week had been pointing all along—down hard. The AI-spending anxiety that first surfaced as after-hours earnings reactions on Wednesday and Thursday became a broad, realized selloff on Friday, as investors stopped treating capital expenditure as a reason to buy and started treating it as a risk to sell. The damage was concentrated in the names that had led all year, with one chipmaker logging its worst day in half a century, while energy and defensive sectors drew the money leaving tech. Underneath the market drama, a record crypto heist added to the risk-off mood. In positioning, the month's resilient-navigation theme kept moving: a national constellation getting rebuilt from the launch pad, and a timing module built to keep critical infrastructure synchronized when the sky gets hostile.

## Tech News

### October Closes With a Brutal Selloff as the AI-Spending Reckoning Goes Broad

Friday turned a two-day earnings wobble into a month-ending rout. The **Nasdaq Composite** fell **2.86%** to **25,437.00**—its worst single day since early June—while the **S&P 500** dropped **1.82%** to **7,580.07** and the **Dow Jones Industrial Average** eased **1.09%** to **51,770.31**, per closing data from Yahoo Finance and TheStreet. The **Russell 2000** slid **1.66%** to **2,811.84**, and the **Cboe Volatility Index** spiked—up more than **30%** intraday before settling near **19.4**—as nearly **70%** of U.S.-listed issues fell. For the month, the Nasdaq shed roughly **5.2%**, its worst October showing since March 2025.

The driver was a shift in how the market reads the AI buildout. After a week in which Alphabet and Amazon posted rare misses and the cash their data-center spending consumes was laid bare, investors recalibrated. "Strong AI demand is no longer enough on its own," analyst **Daniela Hathorn** said, arguing that companies now have to show capital spending is translating into revenue growth, margins, and free cash flow. Two Federal Reserve officials added to the unease by suggesting rates may still need to rise, and the Fed's preferred inflation gauge showed prices heated up in September—pushing the **10-year Treasury yield** to **4.917%**. The **S&P 500 technology sector** fell nearly **5%**, and the rotation was unmistakable: money moved out of the crowded megacaps and into the rate-sensitive and defensive corners that had lagged.

### Update: Intel Logs Its Worst Day Since 1974 on a Weak Outlook and a Factory Accident

The after-hours plunge flagged on Thursday turned into a historic single-session collapse. **Intel** cratered roughly **29%** on Friday—its worst day since **1974**—after guiding fourth-quarter revenue to just **$15.0 billion to $16.0 billion**, a sequential decline that badly undershot Wall Street, per TheStreet and StockTitan. The genuinely new detail beyond the guidance already covered was the cause: management cited a severe PC slump tied to surging memory-chip prices that it expects to persist, and a **factory accident in Israel** that disrupted operations, prompting at least one analyst downgrade.

The frustration is that Intel's data-center business is finally catching the AI wave even as the rest of the company stalls—but on Friday investors judged the guidance, not the bright spot. The selloff spread across silicon: the **VanEck Semiconductor ETF** dropped **7.87%**, with **AMD** down **12.02%** and **Nvidia** off **4.95%**. **Reddit**, another name whose soft after-hours guidance was noted Thursday, saw the reaction realized in full, falling about **28%** in Friday's regular session.

### A Record $6.3 Billion Hack Hits Binance, the Largest Crypto Heist Ever

The day's other shock came from outside the equity market. Crypto exchange **Binance** suffered a **$6.3 billion** hack—described as the largest crypto heist on record—sending **Bitcoin** down **3.33%** to about **$77,283**, per TheStreet. The company said roughly **$3 billion** in assets had been frozen and pledged to cover all customer losses, but the breach rippled through risk assets at exactly the wrong moment.

The fallout hit the publicly traded crypto complex hard, with **Circle**, **Coinbase**, **Robinhood**, and **Strategy** each down more than **6%** on the session. Coming on a day when concerns about AI-related hacks were already weighing on software names, the Binance breach reinforced a broader anxiety: that the infrastructure underpinning the year's hottest trades—AI compute and digital assets alike—carries operational risks the market had been happy to overlook while prices climbed.

## GNSS News

### India Launches NVS-03 to Rebuild Its NavIC Navigation Constellation

The freshest positioning story lifted off from a launch pad. Late on **October 30**, India's space agency **ISRO** was set to launch its **NVS-03** navigation satellite aboard a **GSLV-F18** rocket from the Second Launch Pad at Sriharikota, with liftoff scheduled for **11:51 p.m. IST**, targeting a geosynchronous transfer orbit, per ISRO's mission brochure and Indian press including Deccan Chronicle and The Hans India. The spacecraft is the third in the second-generation NVS series and a key step in restoring full service to **NavIC**, India's regional satellite navigation system.

The satellite is built for both resilience and wider reach. NVS-03 carries tri-band navigation signals in the **L1, L5, and S bands**—the newly added **L1** signal improving compatibility with mass-market receivers, faster position fixes, and lower power draw in smartphones—and an indigenous **rubidium atomic clock** for timing accuracy and continuity, replacing the imported clocks that troubled earlier satellites. The mission matters because NavIC has been working to recover from a setback: the previous **NVS-02** satellite suffered a post-launch orbit-raising shortfall when its thrusters failed to fire, leaving a gap the constellation needs to fill as older spacecraft age. NavIC's signals increasingly underpin civilian systems—AIS-140 vehicle trackers mandated to support it, drone geofencing, and timing for payments, trading, and telecom networks—making the restoration more than a strategic exercise. (The launch outcome was still being confirmed at press time.)

### u-blox Unveils the All-Band ZED-X20T Secure Timing Module

The complementary story targeted the clocks that keep infrastructure in sync. On **October 29**, Swiss positioning specialist **u-blox** launched the **ZED-X20T**, a new flagship GNSS timing module aimed at communication networks, data centers, and power grids, per the company's announcement via TradingView. It extends the firm's all-band X20 platform into timing and ships in the established **17 × 22 mm** ZED form factor, pin-compatible with the widely deployed ZED-F9T to give existing customers a drop-in upgrade path.

The engineering is built around accuracy that survives interference. The ZED-X20T receives the **L1, L2, L5, and L6** bands across all constellations to better suppress ionospheric error and ride out disrupted spectrum, and it pulls **Galileo High Accuracy Service (HAS)** corrections directly over the **E6** signal with no separate receiver, internet link, or subscription—delivering timing accuracy better than **2.0 nanoseconds** (1-sigma) with HAS. On the security side it pairs **secure boot** and **authenticated GNSS navigation data** with advanced interference detection that flags suspicious signals to protect timing integrity. "Trusted synchronization is becoming increasingly important to service continuity as communication and energy infrastructure grows more distributed," said **Samuli Pietilä**, u-blox's director of product management for timing and infrastructure GNSS. Engineering samples are planned for **December 2026**.
