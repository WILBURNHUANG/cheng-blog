Thursday captured the AI industry arguing with itself in real time. On one stage, Meta made the most aggressive bet yet that the future of AI is a gadget you wear, not an app you open—and the market rewarded it with a $2 trillion valuation. On another, the people building the technology kept warning that it's moving too fast: a top Microsoft executive drew a "red line" on human control, and a cybersecurity rally born from a model Anthropic decided was too dangerous to ship rolled on. Even the infrastructure story turned defensive, with Oracle reaching for a contract clause to protect itself from a data center it can't get built. In positioning, the theme was the quieter, steadier one of the year—assume the signal will be attacked—expressed this time through better antennas and a market report that made resilience the headline.

## Tech News

### Meta Turns Muse Into Hardware: a Pocket AI "Charm" and $1,299 VR Glasses

The day's biggest news was Meta betting that the AI agent everyone downloaded should become something you carry. At **Meta Connect 2026**, CEO **Mark Zuckerberg** unveiled the **Muse Charm**, a pocket-sized device—about the size of an AirPods case, with a roughly **2-inch touchscreen** and a built-in **5G connection**—that gives users a direct line to their Muse AI agent without unlocking a phone or opening an app, per Bloomberg, CNBC, and CNN. It's slated to ship in time for the holidays in **December**, with no price announced, though people familiar said it would land in smartwatch territory.

The Charm didn't arrive alone. Meta also debuted **$1,299 VR Glasses** and new camera-free Ray-Ban options, a hardware blitz aimed at making Muse "part of your everyday life," per CNN. The strategic read is clear: after Muse topped the app charts, Meta wants to escape the phone entirely and own the device layer where agents live. It's the same wager Humane and others lost on—that people want a dedicated AI gadget—but made by a company with the distribution and the balance sheet to force the market to find out.

### Meta Nears a $2 Trillion Valuation on Its Best Month Since 2013

The market put a number on the Muse frenzy. **Meta's shares have jumped roughly 36% in September**, on pace for the company's **best month since July 2013** and leaving it about **1% away** from a **$2 trillion** market value—an elite tier occupied by only a handful of companies, per Bloomberg. The rally has muffled the year's loudest worry: that Meta's enormous AI capital spending wouldn't pay off.

The significance is the reversal in sentiment. For much of 2026 the concern hanging over the megacap AI names was whether the buildout would ever earn its cost; a consumer hit that vaulted to the top of the charts is exactly the kind of tangible, revenue-adjacent proof investors had been waiting for. Whether Muse actually converts downloads into durable profit is unproven—but for now, a single successful app has done what quarters of infrastructure promises couldn't, and re-rated one of the largest companies on earth in a matter of weeks.

### Microsoft's Suleyman Says AI Needs a "Red Line": Human Control

The sharpest counterpoint to the exuberance came from inside the industry. Microsoft AI chief **Mustafa Suleyman** argued that the race to build ever more powerful AI needs a firm red line—**AI must remain under human control**—rejecting the idea that superintelligence exceeding that control is inevitable, per Bloomberg. He also insisted competition with China shouldn't be an excuse to skip safeguards: "I don't think we should use China as the bogeyman for not making progress on our own efforts" on safety.

The context makes it pointed. Microsoft recently released a draft AI **code of conduct** requiring its models to never resist a shutdown order, taking a visibly harder line than rivals even as the White House resists broad regulation. Coming days after Anthropic's Dario Amodei called for pacing frontier development—and the same week two labs launched a price war doing the opposite—Suleyman's stance sharpens a genuine split among the people building the technology over whether the current pace is safe, and who should be the one to slow it.

### Oracle Invokes "Force Majeure" to Shield Itself on a Troubled New Mexico Data Center

The infrastructure story turned defensive. **Oracle** sent a **force majeure** notice to the developer of its **Project Jupiter** data center in New Mexico—a unit of **Blue Owl Capital**—seeking to delay payment if the project doesn't come online in **2028**, and the stock fell about **4%**, per Bloomberg, CNBC, and TechCrunch. The move is a rare admission that regulatory and permitting headwinds can stall even a flagship AI campus.

The obstacles have piled up. A gas pipeline meant to feed the site has slipped nearly six months to **February 2027**, an air-quality permit for its fuel-cell power system is pending against a **November 23** deadline, and local opposition ahead of the midterms continues to mount—even after the state Supreme Court cleared some challenges on September 17. Investors read the notice as Oracle pricing in execution and credit risk on AI infrastructure, a reminder that the buildout's tightest constraint is increasingly permits and power, not chips.

**Additional Headlines:**

- **Cybersecurity stocks keep soaring on the Mythos aftershock**: A Goldman Sachs basket of cybersecurity names has **more than doubled since an April 10 low**, with **CrowdStrike, Palo Alto Networks, and Fortinet** each up over **130%**, after Anthropic restricted its **Mythos** model—deemed too powerful to release safely—over fears it could power cyberattacks, per Bloomberg. Investors are now questioning how long the rally can last.
- **Anthropic locks Mythos behind "Project Glasswing"**: Rather than ship Mythos, Anthropic built a restricted-access framework involving **12 major corporations**—including Amazon, Microsoft, and Apple—and **40 institutions**, per Cybersecurity Ventures, an unusual model for gating a frontier system deemed unsafe for public release.
- **AI-enabled attacks jumped 89% in 2025**: The surge underpinning the security trade is real—AI-assisted intrusions rose **89%** year over year, per CrowdStrike—after a coalition of 100-plus tech and financial firms warned in August that AI-powered cyberattacks are about to get far harder to stop.

## GNSS News

### Calian Expands Its Resilient Antenna Portfolio as Anti-Jamming Goes Mainstream

The freshest positioning hardware news came from the antenna side of the resilience push. **Calian** strengthened its precision-GNSS lineup with three new models unveiled at **INTERGEO 2026** in Munich: the **Accutenna 4** (AC4990PXF) survey antenna, a new pole-mount **controlled reception pattern antenna (CRPA)**, and the **AJ979XF+-MAR** full-band anti-jamming antenna, targeting surveying, critical infrastructure, marine, autonomy, and defense users, per Inside GNSS and GPS World.

The standout is the anti-jamming unit. The **AJ979XF+-MAR** covers **GPS, Galileo, BeiDou, GLONASS, QZSS, NavIC, and regional SBAS**, pairing Calian's Low Elevation Angle Nulling Antenna (LEANA) design with its extended filtering (XF+) to deliver **more than 80 dB of out-of-band rejection** against LTE and satellite-communications interference. It's a telling sign of where the market has moved: features once reserved for military CRPAs—nulling and heavy filtering—are now being packaged for commercial marine and infrastructure buyers, because jamming has stopped being a battlefield-only problem.

### Keysight's PNT Report 2026 Names "Protect, Toughen, Augment" the Year's Dominant Theme

The complementary story was a read on the whole market. **Keysight's PNT Report 2026** frames a positioning, navigation, and timing landscape reshaped by resilient architectures, LEO PNT, autonomy, sovereign systems, and new threats, per Inside GNSS. Its central finding: **protecting, toughening, and augmenting PNT** is now a dominant trend, as more systems fuse GNSS with inertial, terrestrial, and alternative sources rather than trusting a single signal.

The report is useful less as news than as a map of where the field is heading. It flags **eight emerging technology areas**—AI and machine learning for hybrid positioning, precise orbit determination in LEO, dedicated PNT for the Moon and Mars, chip-scale atomic clocks, advanced CRPA technology, quantum inertial navigation, satellite and private-5G positioning, and PNT woven into future 6G sensing. Taken together with the month's steady stream of quantum-sensor and authentication demonstrations, it confirms the industry has settled on a single organizing principle: resilience isn't a feature anymore, it's the whole product roadmap.
