The midterms rewrote the trade. Democrats took both chambers of Congress, and a market that had spent two weeks agonizing over AI capital spending decided a divided-no-more Washington might finally do something about it. The result was a split screen: a thunderous rally in the broad market and the software and consumer megacaps, and a sharp selloff in the data-center and AI-cloud names most exposed to a regulatory crackdown. Oil, meanwhile, cratered, gold and silver ran, and the after-hours session served up another round of winners and losers. In positioning, the month's resilience theme kept its momentum—this time in the low-Earth-orbit signals and test benches being built to keep receivers working when GPS goes dark.

## Tech News

### The Democratic Sweep Sends the Dow to a Record—but Splits the AI Trade in Two

Tuesday's election result detonated under the market. Democrats won control of both the **House and Senate**, and [Bloomberg reported](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-04/big-tech-stocks-soar-as-traders-bet-democrats-will-slow-ai-spend) that Big Tech soared on a bet that Democrats will "save AI spenders from themselves"—reining in the breakneck data-center buildout that investors had come to see as a cash drain. The **Dow Jones Industrial Average** surged **3.03%**, up **1,569 points** to a record **53,356.12**, while the **S&P 500** climbed **1.58%** to **7,688.62** and the **Nasdaq Composite** rose **1.44%** to **25,736.51**, per CNBC's closing wrap. The **Russell 2000** added **1.58%** to **2,856.96**.

But the rally was lopsided in a telling way. The megacaps that spend on AI rather than sell the hardware jumped—**Apple** rose **5.30%** to an intraday record of **$354.33**, **Amazon** gained **6.18%**, and **Microsoft** closed sharply higher. The names that live or die by data-center capex went the other way: **CoreWeave** closed down **9.94%** and **Nvidia** fell **3.14%**, with **Super Micro** off **5.73%**. The fear crystallized overnight when Democrat **Gina Hinojosa** won the Texas governor's race on a pledge to pause new data-center construction, and Nasdaq-100 futures bottomed near **27,615** before recovering. **Trump Media** slumped **24.45%** in the risk reshuffle.

### Oil Collapses More Than 8% as Havens Catch a Bid

The commodity complex staged its own dramatic move. **U.S. crude** fell more than **8%** on the session, with Brent sliding below **$100** a barrel for the first time in weeks, extending the reversal of the war premium that had built up on Strait of Hormuz tensions. Energy stocks logged the steepest sector declines of the day.

Money that left oil and risk flowed into classic safe havens. **Gold futures** jumped nearly **2.9%** toward **$4,053** an ounce and **silver** leapt almost **5%** past **$52**, while investors bid up **U.S. Treasuries**, pushing yields lower—a flight-to-safety posture sitting oddly alongside a record close in the Dow. The divergence captured the day's mood: euphoria in equities, caution everywhere else.

### After the Bell, AppLovin Soars and Qualcomm Stumbles

The earnings tape stayed noisy into the evening. **AppLovin** rocketed **23.52%** in extended trading on a strong report, the standout of the session, while adtech peer sentiment lifted alongside it. On the other side, **Qualcomm** fell **7.18%** after hours despite the chipmaker's results, a reminder that even in a rally investors are quick to punish any crack in guidance.

The travel and social names took their lumps too. **Expedia** dropped **7.69%** and **Snap** slid **7.60%** after the close, while **Arm Holdings** was little changed, down **0.58%**. The split between AppLovin's surge and Qualcomm's slide underscored a market still sorting winners from losers one print at a time, even as the indexes roared.

**Additional Headlines:**

- **Nvidia pulls back on a China twist**: Beyond the data-center selloff, the Financial Times reported (via Bloomberg) that **Nvidia** has told data-center clients to **trim their China business**, adding another wrinkle to a stock already down **3.14%** on the day.
- **Fidelity National Information Services craters**: **FIS** tumbled roughly **24.7%** intraday, one of the day's worst single-name blowups outside the AI complex.
- **Duolingo extends its run**: **Duolingo** gained again, up more than **19%** in premarket on momentum from its user-growth beat.
- **Amdocs keeps falling**: **Amdocs** remained deep in the red, down about **36%** in premarket after its weak fiscal 2027 outlook.
- **Asian chips wobble**: Japan's **Nikkei 225** closed down **1.42%** as Asian semiconductor names fell on the AI-regulation jitters that rippled out of Washington.

## GNSS News

### Xona Opens "Pulsar Verified" to Certify Receivers for Its LEO Navigation Signals

The freshest positioning story is about building an ecosystem, not just a constellation. On **November 3**, **Xona Space Systems** launched **Pulsar Verified**, a partnership program that lets receiver makers, chipset integrators and test-equipment developers formally certify that their hardware works with Xona's **Pulsar low-Earth-orbit (LEO) navigation signals**, with verification performed by the engineers who designed the signals, per GPS World. The aim is to speed LEO-PNT adoption through the existing commercial GNSS supply chain rather than forcing the industry to start from scratch.

The inaugural wave of partners reads like a who's-who of the positioning world: **Trimble** (bringing devices dating back to 2018 into Pulsar compatibility), **Septentrio** (now part of Hexagon, integrating Pulsar across its next-generation lineup after joining Xona's Series C), plus **STMicroelectronics**, **Safran**, **StarNav** and **Keysight**. The business case is resilience: Xona says its stronger LEO signals can shrink a jammer's effective area by up to **95%**, and its **Pulsar-0** demonstration satellite has now completed a year in orbit, logging more than **350 transmission passes** across four continents and terabytes of observation data. "In live-sky jamming tests in several countries, we watched as GNSS went dark but Pulsar held," said co-founder and CTO **Tyler Reid**, framing the program as a way to "build for the service we'll offer tomorrow, today."

### Rohde & Schwarz Details How It Puts GNSS Receivers Through a Jamming Gauntlet

The complementary story came from the test bench. In a **November 4** interview with Inside GNSS at **ION GNSS+ 2026**, Rohde & Schwarz application engineer **Paul Shaffer** described how the company's signal generators let receiver developers probe GNSS performance under controlled interference—simulating a scenario such as urban driving while injecting jamming, then dialing the interference level up until a supplied **u-blox** receiver loses its position fix. The point is to find exactly where resilience breaks, under repeatable conditions.

The hardware spans a flagship and a workhorse. The **R&S SMW200A** supports multi-constellation, multi-frequency and multi-antenna scenarios and can be combined in phase-coherent setups for multi-channel tests—including competing GNSS signals—while the more compact **R&S SMBV100B** handles receiver characterization and production testing. Both can replay recorded I/Q data or stream custom interference waveforms, and the same instruments reach into radar and electronic-warfare signals. "We can adjust the level of that interference signal and see how the receiver responds," Shaffer said. "Flexibility is where we stand out, for sure."
