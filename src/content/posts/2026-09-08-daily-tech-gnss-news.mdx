Tuesday's news kept turning on the same two decisions: what to buy and what to refuse. Qualcomm landed its biggest data-center customer yet and got a hyperscaler to underwrite the bet in stock. Mistral pulled in Europe's largest tech round to stay at the table. Anthropic looked hard at a $6 billion deal and walked. And two governments pushed in opposite directions on the AI economy—Beijing pledging half a trillion dollars to build compute, Brussels forcing Google to degrade the product 450 million people use. In positioning, the throughline was integration: Europe's receiver industry moving to fold low-Earth-orbit signals into ordinary GNSS chips rather than treat them as an experiment.

## Tech News

### Qualcomm Signs Amazon as Its First Big Cloud Customer for Custom AI Chips

**Qualcomm** agreed to build custom AI data-center chips for **Amazon Web Services** across multiple generations of silicon, its first major Western hyperscaler and a direct move onto **Nvidia's** turf, per Bloomberg. Qualcomm shares jumped about **10%** on the news. The partnership centers on **inference**—running trained models rather than training them—where Qualcomm's mobile heritage in power efficiency is its pitch, and it extends into **optical networking** gear used to lash together the servers inside AI clusters.

The structure is the tell. Qualcomm issued warrants letting Amazon acquire **25 million** shares at **$161.26** each—up to about **$4 billion** of stock—with issuance tied to Amazon buying as much as **$60 billion** of Qualcomm products over the life of the deal, per CNBC. In other words, the customer's upside is welded to how much silicon it actually orders. For a company still known mostly for phone modems, getting AWS to both buy the chips and take equity is the clearest sign yet that the data-center diversification is real.

### Mistral Raises €3 Billion at a €21 Billion Valuation in Europe's Largest Tech Round

French AI lab **Mistral** raised **€3 billion** in a Series D that values it above **€21 billion**, the largest private technology round ever completed in Europe, per TechStartups. **Samsung** led the financing, joined by the EU-backed **Scaleup Europe Fund**, PSG Equity, and existing investor **ASML**. Mistral's annual recurring revenue has reportedly crossed **$1 billion**, putting it past the research-lab phase and into commercial contention against OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, and Meta.

The round is as much geopolitics as finance. Europe has spent years fretting about its dependence on American and Chinese AI platforms, and a heavily capitalized Mistral—built around open-weight models and data-sovereign enterprise deployments—gives governments and companies a homegrown option. The continent still faces a large compute gap with the U.S., but the Samsung-led check shows investors are willing to fund a European contender at global scale.

### Anthropic Walks Away From a $6 Billion Decart Acquisition After Due Diligence

**Anthropic** ended talks to acquire Israeli AI startup **Decart** after conducting due diligence, abandoning what would have been roughly a **$6 billion** deal, per Bloomberg. Decart builds software that makes chips run AI models more efficiently, and Anthropic's offer—structured mostly in stock at about a **50%** premium to Decart's last private valuation—was attractive enough that Decart's founders had earlier turned down a higher cash offer from **Nvidia**, preferring Anthropic shares ahead of its planned IPO at a reported **$2 trillion** valuation.

The restraint is the story. With capital sloshing toward anything AI-adjacent, a frontier lab looking hard at a multibillion-dollar acquisition and then declining is a rarer signal than another mega-round. The two may still work together—Anthropic could become a customer or investor—but for now the takeaway is that even the buyers with the deepest pockets are willing to say no.

**Additional Headlines:**

- **China targets a $532 billion compute buildout**: Beijing's new five-year plan calls for **3.8 trillion yuan (about $532 billion)** of information-infrastructure investment through 2030 and aims to more than quadruple "intelligent computing" capacity to **9,800 eflops**, explicitly optimized around domestically made accelerators to route around U.S. export controls, per the South China Morning Post.
- **Google says its EU Search is now worse by design**: Alphabet rolled out redesigned European results to satisfy the Digital Markets Act after a **€460 million** fine, promoting rival search engines and stripping features like real-time hotel and flight prices; a senior Google VP called it the "largest reduction in quality of service" in Search's 29-year history, per Reuters.
- **ASML and Intel push High-NA EUV forward**: ASML is developing larger photomasks to let its most advanced High-NA lithography print bigger AI dies, promising roughly **40%** more throughput, while Intel says it has already processed more than **one million** wafers on High-NA systems, per Reuters and Tom's Hardware.
- **The AI boom is quietly raising phone prices**: Surging demand for high-bandwidth memory in AI servers is pulling capacity away from ordinary DRAM, and with Samsung, SK Hynix, and Micron controlling about **90%** of the market, some memory prices have risen severalfold—costs device makers will pass to buyers, per The Verge.
- **Stoke Space raises $1 billion to chase reusable rockets**: The U.S. launch startup closed a **$1 billion** Series E co-led by Point72 Ventures and Spark Capital—bringing total funding to about **$2.3 billion**—to build a fully reusable Nova rocket aimed squarely at SpaceX, per TechCrunch.

## GNSS News

### Europe's Receiver Makers Move to Fold LEO-PNT Signals Into Ordinary GNSS Chips

The low-Earth-orbit positioning story is shifting from orbital demos to receiver integration, and Europe's chip vendors are now doing the plumbing. Belgian firm **Septentrio** reported early tracking of a next-generation LEO-PNT satellite and signed a memorandum to accelerate joint testing aimed at validating **centimeter-level** performance and better indoor availability, treating the new signals as another layer inside its GNSS stack rather than a standalone add-on, per Inside GNSS. The move matters because LEO signals arrive far stronger than traditional mid-orbit GNSS, offering a harder-to-jam complement for the same receivers already sold into survey, robotics, and autonomy.

Making that practical takes test infrastructure, and that piece fell into place too: **Safran** certified its **Skydel** simulator to emulate LEO-PNT waveforms, letting receiver makers and test labs run multi-constellation LEO scenarios—blending U.S. commercial and European demonstrator signals—in a single environment before touching real hardware. Alongside Europe's own **Synchrocube** in-orbit demonstrator, the pieces now line up in timing, format, and authentication design, moving LEO-PNT from a resilience talking point toward something integrators can actually validate and ship.

## Key Takeaways

- **AI's supply chain is being locked in with equity, not just contracts**: Qualcomm won Amazon as its first big cloud customer for custom inference chips—with warrants tied to as much as $60 billion of orders—while Mistral raised Europe's largest-ever tech round at a €21 billion valuation, each stitching customers and backers directly into the bet.
- **Discipline and industrial policy cut against the frenzy**: Anthropic walked away from a $6 billion Decart acquisition after due diligence, even as China pledged $532 billion to quadruple national compute by 2030 and Google was forced to degrade its own European Search to satisfy the Digital Markets Act.
- **LEO-PNT is graduating from orbit to the receiver**: Septentrio began tracking new low-Earth-orbit positioning signals and signed on for joint validation, while Safran's Skydel simulator can now emulate those waveforms—letting Europe's GNSS chipmakers fold a harder-to-jam signal layer into hardware they already ship.
