Wednesday's news was a study in what happens when a call for restraint meets a market that won't slow down. A day after Anthropic's Dario Amodei publicly argued for pacing frontier AI, his own company and OpenAI shipped dueling models priced to undercut each other—the opposite of a slowdown. Underneath the headline race, the capital quietly rotated from training to inference, where the durable business of actually running models is now drawing venture money at eye-watering valuations, and Alibaba extended its infrastructure bet across two more continents. In positioning, the through-line held—assume the signal will be denied—with two fresh answers aimed at the hardest cases: a car that navigates urban canyons without clean GNSS, and a soldier's phone that maps a squad's movements with no satellites at all.

## Tech News

### Anthropic Ships Opus 5.5, OpenAI Counters an Hour Later, and a Price War Breaks Out

The day's defining event was a one-two punch. **Anthropic** released **Claude Opus 5.5**, a flagship it says matches its top-end Fable model on most complex tasks at a **20% lower price** and runs about **40% cheaper** than Opus 5, with input dropping to **$4** per million tokens and output to **$20**, per Bloomberg and CNBC. Roughly an hour later, **OpenAI** shipped two models— **GPT-6 Sol** and **GPT-6 Luna**—priced to undercut it, with Luna landing at **$0.10/$0.50** per million tokens, among OpenAI's cheapest ever, per SiliconANGLE and coverage collated by Simon Willison.

The timing is the story. Both releases were the labs' first major launches since Anthropic CEO **Dario Amodei** publicly advocated pacing frontier development so safety can keep up—and both did the opposite, cutting prices to win share. Fortune framed it bluntly: *what slowdown?* The competition is also being squeezed from below by cheaper open-weight models, many from China, which is exactly what makes a price war rational and painful at once: it drives the cost of intelligence down for customers while eroding the margins of the companies burning hundreds of billions to build it.

### The Money Moves to Inference: Modal and Baseten Raise at $15B and $26B

The clearest read on where AI value is migrating came from two funding talks. **Modal Labs** is in discussions to raise at roughly a **$15 billion** valuation—about triple its worth four months ago—while **Baseten** is in talks for a round that could value it at **$26 billion**, up from **$13 billion** in June, per Bloomberg. Both companies sell platforms that help businesses run AI models in production, and both are riding surging demand for the same thing: inference, the work of operating a model after it's been trained.

The rotation matters as much as the numbers. For two years the capital and the headlines chased training—bigger clusters, bigger frontier runs—but by some estimates spending on inference compute is now set to eclipse training as adoption broadens and agentic tools consume far more tokens per task. Valuations doubling in a matter of months for "picks and shovels" inference firms is the market pricing in that shift: the durable business may be less in building the smartest model and more in running everyone's models cheaply and reliably at scale.

### Update: Alibaba Extends Its 20-Gigawatt Buildout Into Europe and the Middle East

A day after unveiling its Zhenwu V900 chip and a 20-gigawatt data-center target, **Alibaba** put geography behind the plan. The company will open its **first cloud regions in Turkey, Finland, and the Netherlands** over the next **12 months**, expanding into Europe and the Middle East as it races the U.S. on both chips and models, per Bloomberg. The move fleshes out the previously announced global target with specific footholds rather than a headline figure.

The new information is the internationalization. Where Tuesday's news was about silicon and a 2032 capacity goal, Wednesday's is about planting Alibaba Cloud regions on foreign soil—putting the company in more direct competition with AWS, Microsoft, and Google in their own backyards, and signaling that China's frontier-AI ambitions now include exporting the infrastructure, not just building it at home.

## GNSS News

### Jaguar Land Rover Demonstrates a Factor-Graph Navigation Unit That Beats Kalman Filtering

The freshest positioning result came from an unexpected place: a carmaker's research lab. **Jaguar Land Rover** built and demonstrated the **FGO-Nav**, a proof-of-concept navigation platform using **factor graph optimization (FGO)** instead of the Kalman filtering that has anchored vehicle positioning for decades, per Inside GNSS. Developed under the European Space Agency's **NAVISP EL1-088** project, the modular unit supports GNSS-only, GNSS/INS, RTK, and PPP modes and was validated across more than **24 hours** of real-world, multi-environment testing.

The payoff shows up exactly where cars struggle. In degraded GNSS conditions—urban canyons, tunnels, intermittent satellite visibility—tightly coupled FGO consistently delivered better accuracy than loosely coupled FGO, Extended Kalman Filtering, and commercial GNSS receivers, meeting all of ESA's requirements. The significance is that FGO re-optimizes across a whole window of past measurements rather than propagating a single best estimate forward, so it recovers gracefully when the signal drops out and returns. For automakers hardening receivers against the year's steady drumbeat of jamming and spoofing, it's evidence that a smarter estimator—not just more sensors—can close much of the gap where satellites go quiet.

### SPARC AI's Overwatch Patrol Tracks Foot Soldiers With No Receiver at All

The complementary answer came from the dismounted side. Canadian firm **SPARC AI** launched **Overwatch Patrol** at Poland's **MSPO 2026** defense expo, a positioning app that lets soldiers and security teams see each other move on a map in real time when GPS is jammed, spoofed, degraded, or simply gone, per GPS World and The Defense Post. The system's headline claim is what it doesn't need: **no external receiver, antenna, added hardware, or software**—it runs on any mobile device as a native app or in a browser, drawing on SPARC AI's cloud-based **Overwatch Positioning Network**.

The design choices are built for the field, not the lab. Teams form on the spot using a dynamic code from the group leader, members join or leave without a central administrator or prior device setup, and any member can drop a shared rally point that the rest can navigate to independently. Where most GPS-denied solutions add a sensor—an inertial unit, a quantum magnetometer, a gravimeter—Overwatch Patrol's bet is that continuous squad awareness can be delivered in software on gear a soldier already carries, keeping the load light while the satellites are unavailable.
