Tuesday's tape kept circling the same question from opposite ends: who profits from the AI buildout, and who pays for it. The bull case got a fresh poster child in an inference-chip startup whose valuation doubled in weeks on the strength of a single customer delivery. The bear case got a courtroom—Meta's youth-harm trial opened as Wall Street started treating an AI political backlash as a real market variable. Between them, Apple offered a glimpse of what all this compute is ultimately for: earbuds that can see. In positioning, the news moved away from the contested-sky headlines of recent weeks toward the unglamorous machinery of military PNT—who owns the receiver programs, and who builds the clocks.

## Tech News

### Etched Raises $700 Million at a $21 Billion Valuation—Doubling in a Month

**Etched**, the inference-chip startup founded by three Harvard dropouts, raised **$700 million** in a round led by **Jane Street** at a **$21 billion** valuation, more than doubling the **$10.3 billion** mark it set just weeks earlier in a July Series C, per TechCrunch and SiliconANGLE. The trajectory is almost vertical: Etched was valued at roughly **$5 billion** in December 2025, and the new round pulled in Kleiner Perkins, Sequoia, Andreessen Horowitz, Tiger Global and Bain Capital Ventures alongside Jane Street.

What separates this raise from the usual AI markup is that a customer voted with its data center. Jane Street—the round's lead—signed on as Etched's first customer shortly after the company's first chips rolled off the line, and installed an Etched-powered rack in its own facility last month, per the company's GlobeNewswire release. Etched sells full "frontier inference clusters" rather than loose silicon, pairing a low-voltage prefill chip with new memory and interconnect technology tuned for the decode stage of inference. In a market obsessed with training, the bet here is that the durable money is in serving models cheaply—and that a specialized ASIC can undercut general-purpose GPUs on cost per token.

### Meta Goes on Trial Against 29 States Over Youth Harm

**Meta** went to trial in federal court in Oakland on Tuesday against a bipartisan coalition of **29 state attorneys general**, in what both sides describe as the largest social-media trial yet, per Courthouse News Service and ABC News. Jury selection opened before U.S. District Judge Yvonne Gonzalez Rogers in a case expected to run six to eight weeks, with the states alleging Meta knowingly designed Facebook and Instagram features to addict children and violated the Children's Online Privacy Protection Act by collecting data on users under 13 without verifiable parental consent.

The stakes are enormous, at least on paper. The states are seeking damages that could, in theory, total as much as **$1.4 trillion**, plus court-ordered changes to how Meta operates its platforms, per the coverage. Jurors are expected to hear testimony from CEO **Mark Zuckerberg**, Instagram chief **Adam Mosseri**, and former Meta employee-turned-whistleblower **Arturo Béjar**. Coming as the industry pours capital into AI-driven engagement, the trial is a reminder that the other half of the "attention economy" story is a mounting legal and regulatory bill.

### Apple Seemingly Leaks Camera-Equipped AirPods With Visual Intelligence

**Apple** appears to have leaked its long-rumored camera-equipped **AirPods** through a promotional video embedded in the **macOS Tahoe 26.7** release candidate, first spotted by MacRumors and quickly amplified across TechCrunch and 9to5Mac. In the clip, a man wearing AirPods Pro holds up a book while Siri narrates: "With Visual Intelligence your world becomes saveable. See something you like? Just ask me to save it for later."

The hardware turns the earbuds into visual sensors. A camera feeds context to Siri's **Visual Intelligence**, letting it identify and log objects in the wearer's surroundings—an ambient, hands-free take on the same "AI that sees what you see" ambition driving smart glasses. Bloomberg's Mark Gurman had reported a late-2027 launch, but a finished promo sitting inside a shipping macOS build suggests Apple could preview the product much sooner, plausibly at next month's iPhone 18 event. For a company that has moved cautiously on generative AI, a wearable that puts a camera on Siri would be its most concrete consumer-AI bet yet.

## GNSS News

### Air Force Takes Full Ownership of the Military M-Code Receiver Programs

The quietly important PNT story is bureaucratic on the surface and consequential underneath. The **Air Force Life Cycle Management Center (AFLCMC)** signed the **Military GPS User Equipment (MGUE)** Transition Support Plan, formally accepting the **MGUE Increment 1 and 2** programs from the **U.S. Space Force** and centralizing them under the **M-Code Aviation Receivers (MAR) Joint Program Office**, per Inside GNSS and SpaceNews. It's the culmination of a handoff that began in late 2024, with acquisition authority moving to the Air Force in December 2025 and program responsibility shifting to a materiel leader at Los Angeles Air Force Base in January 2026.

The point of all this reorganization is jam- and spoof-resistant GPS. MGUE receivers are built to hold position in contested electromagnetic environments, and **Increment 2** is meant to deliver smaller, faster **ASIC** chip cards that can finally exploit the more powerful **M-code** signal broadcast by the newest **GPS III Follow-On** satellites. The program has been dogged by delays, but Air Force officials now expect operational testing of the chip cards for maritime and aviation platforms to wrap up in **fiscal 2027**, per Breaking Defense—putting a date on when the U.S. fleet's most resilient GPS actually reaches the field. After a year of civil-jamming alarm, this is the supply-side answer: the hardened receivers meant to keep military platforms navigating when the signal is under attack.

### Frequency Electronics Wins a Follow-On to Build Atomic Clocks for the Moon

On the timing side—the "T" in PNT that rarely makes headlines—**Frequency Electronics** was awarded a follow-on production contract worth about **$8 million** to supply compact, high-precision **atomic clocks** for position, navigation and timing on lunar missions, per the company's announcement. Precise onboard time is the backbone of any navigation system, and extending that capability to cislunar space is a prerequisite for the lunar PNT architecture that NASA and its partners are assembling. It's a small contract with an outsized signal: the same timing discipline that anchors terrestrial GNSS is now being productized for the Moon, where there is no GPS to fall back on.
