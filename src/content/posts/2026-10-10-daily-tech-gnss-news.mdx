This week's news circled back to the question that has shadowed the whole year: is the AI buildout's revenue real enough to justify the money pouring into it? The clearest jolt came from the company at the center of the boom admitting its sales run rate is well short of what it had implied, a disclosure that rippled straight into chip and cloud stocks and sank an Australian data-center IPO the same week. Washington, meanwhile, turned its immigration crackdown on the tech industry's hiring pipeline, and Apple set a date to sell hardware the old-fashioned way. In positioning, the steady lesson held once more—assume the satellite signal can be jammed or spoofed—expressed this time through a retrofit anti-jam antenna and a new line of modules built to reach the machines that need resilient GNSS most.

## Tech News

### OpenAI's Revenue Run Rate Comes In ~$20 Billion Below Its Own Signal, Rattling AI Stocks

The week's defining story was a number that came in smaller than expected. **OpenAI** told investors its annualized revenue run rate for September was roughly **$50 billion**—about **$20 billion** below the "approaching **$70 billion**" figure it had indicated at a separate event a month earlier, according to Reuters reporting cited by the Irish Times. A source attributed much of the discrepancy to an attempt to compare OpenAI's numbers against rival **Anthropic**, which is targeting **$100 billion** by year-end.

The reaction was immediate and broad. The disclosure pushed down shares of **Nvidia, CoreWeave, Oracle, and Nebius** on Thursday, per SiliconANGLE, as investors who have watched the sector run on ever-larger financing commitments took the shortfall as evidence the revenue underneath may not be keeping pace. It landed the same week SoftBank completed a **$30 billion** investment in OpenAI, funded partly by an **$11.1 billion** bond sale—exactly the kind of leverage that makes a revenue miss reverberate well beyond the company that reported it. After a month dominated by record hardware profits and record debt deals, this was the counterweight: a reminder that the paying-customer side of the equation is still being proven.

### Washington Suspends Microsoft and Big IT Outsourcers From the Green-Card Program

The administration's immigration fight reached directly into tech hiring. The White House suspended **eight firms**—including **Microsoft, Adobe, Cognizant, Infosys, TCS, Wipro, HCL, and Capgemini**—from the **PERM** green-card certification program, the process companies use to sponsor foreign employees for permanent residency, per Tech Startups. H-1B visas themselves are not affected by the suspension. Vice President **JD Vance** accused Microsoft of fraud, framing the move as part of a broader push to prioritize American workers.

The significance is where the pressure is being applied. By targeting the employment-based green-card pipeline rather than temporary visas, the action hits the long-term retention mechanism that lets tech and IT-services firms convert skilled hires into permanent staff—and it names some of the largest users of that system at once. For an industry already navigating a tighter H-1B posture this year, the message is that the government intends to make the path from recruitment to permanent residency materially harder, with the biggest employers singled out first.

### Apple Makes Its October 22 iPad and Mac Event Official

Amid the AI financing drama, Apple returned attention to physical products. **Apple** sent press invitations confirming an event on **October 22**, per TechCrunch, its second product showcase of the fall after last month's iPhone launch. The company is widely expected to unveil an **OLED iPad mini** and a **touchscreen MacBook Pro**, a notable reversal for a company that has long resisted putting touch displays on its laptops.

The lineup reads as a quiet bet that hardware refreshes still move the needle even in an AI-saturated news cycle. An OLED upgrade brings the iPad mini in line with the display technology already across Apple's premium tablets, while a touchscreen MacBook Pro would mark one of the Mac's biggest interface shifts in years. Reports suggest Apple may stagger the fall launches across more than one event, a sign of how many products it is trying to refresh before the holiday quarter.

## GNSS News

### NAL Technologies Launches a Plug-In Anti-Jam Antenna Built Around Iridium's Signal

The freshest positioning news was a way to harden gear already in the field without replacing it. **NAL Technologies (Naltec)** launched **PntGuard Syntrx**, a smart-antenna upgrade that protects GNSS/GPS timing and positioning in existing equipment, on **October 8**, with commercial shipping set to begin in **December**, per Inside GNSS. The plug-in unit detects interference automatically and draws on a secure **Iridium PNT** signal that Naltec says is roughly **1,000 times stronger** than GPS and resistant to jamming and spoofing, adding an assured-PNT (APNT) layer so operations can continue through disruption.

The move matters because it treats resilience as a retrofit rather than a redesign. The target users—tactical radios, satcom terminals, and electronic-warfare platforms on the defense side, and financial institutions, energy-grid operators, and telecom networks on the civilian side—all depend on accurate timing that is the single most jamming-exposed link in the PNT stack. By delivering a second, harder-to-jam source in a unit that bolts onto hardware already deployed (the company says its APNT technology is now in more than **15,000** devices worldwide), Naltec is pushing layered timing into the installed base instead of waiting for a forklift upgrade. As NAL president **Robert Bills** put it, "nearly every fielded network, terminal, and weapon system depends on accurate PNT."

### Fibocom Rolls Out Its MGB Series GNSS Modules—With a NavIC Variant—at India Mobile Congress

The complementary story was about getting resilient positioning into the fast-growing IoT market. **Fibocom** introduced its **MGB Series** GNSS module portfolio at **India Mobile Congress 2026** in New Delhi on **October 7**, per a PR Newswire release. The lineup spans single-frequency modules (**MGB180** and **MGB280**, supporting GPS, BDS, GLONASS, Galileo, and QZSS with 96 tracking channels) aimed at cost-sensitive asset tracking, fleet management, and telematics, up to the dual-frequency **MGB390** series with 180 channels—including an **MGB390-AR** variant offering **RTK** accuracy of **1 cm + 1 ppm** and an **MGB390-A** that supports **NavIC**, India's regional navigation system. All three are in mass production.

The significance is the market the line is tuned for. Tailoring a tiered module family across cost, power, and accuracy points—and adding NavIC support specifically for India—targets the integration problem that gates autonomy and IoT adoption: a capable receiver only helps if developers can match it to a device's budget and the local satellite constellations it can actually use. As Fibocom's **Kony Li** framed it, India's growing IoT ecosystem needs reliable positioning options at every price and performance level. Against a year spent hardening GNSS against interference, this is the other half of the job—spreading precise positioning across the high-volume platforms multiplying fastest in emerging markets.
